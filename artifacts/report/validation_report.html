Scope

Intended use. Monthly scoring of open credit card accounts that are current, using the bank's own deposit and card records, to (1) flag accounts likely to reach 60 or more days past due within six months, (2) flag accounts likely to file for bankruptcy within six months, and (3) recommend one of four account-management actions, or none, for flagged accounts. Recommendations go to a person or a governed decision process; the scorer changes nothing on an account.

Not intended for origination or line-increase decisions, accounts that are already past due, collections prioritisation, pricing, or any product other than revolving card accounts.

Conceptual soundness

Both scores are sums of one lookup table per feature. A table is the fold of every depth-one tree that split on that feature, so nothing is approximated when the model is read, scored or explained. Each feature carries a declared direction in the feature registry; training enforces it, this report re-checks it, and the scorer refuses to load a bundle that breaks it.

Restricted feature

Adding the legal-services merchant-category flag changes AUC by +0.0000 (delinquency) and +0.0007 (bankruptcy). It is excluded from the shipped models. A feature flagging receipt of government benefits is prohibited in the registry and is never computed.

Reason codes

Reasons are the feature groups that add the most risk relative to the average account, taken straight from the scoring tables, at most four, one per group. This follows a method described in the official commentary to Regulation B for scored decisions (12 CFR 1002.9(b)(2), comment 5). When a value is absent and its absence adds risk, the reason says so.

Limitations

  • All data are simulated. Lift, lead time and intervention effects are properties of the simulator's assumptions and will differ on a real book.
  • The simulated bankruptcy rate is set above what a typical card portfolio sees so that the test window contains enough filings to measure anything.
  • The champion stand-in has no credit-bureau data. Measured lift over a bank's actual behaviour score will be smaller.
  • The models are additive. Interactions between features are not captured; the benchmark comparison bounds what that costs here, not on other data.
  • Action recommendations rest on a randomized pilot. Without one on real accounts, the recommendation layer cannot be fitted or validated, and historical collector decisions are not a substitute.
  • Recommendations are only made inside the population the pilot enrolled. Alerted accounts outside it get no recommendation.
  • Intervention value counts interest, interchange and cash-advance fees and excludes penalty fees. A bank that values these differently will get a different policy.
  • Calibration is fitted on one window. The report shows it moving under a simulated downturn; a production deployment needs a recalibration trigger tied to the observed-over-predicted ratio.
  • Outcome windows are six months for the scores and eighteen for action value. Effects that appear later are not measured.
